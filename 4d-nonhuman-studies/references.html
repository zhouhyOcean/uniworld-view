Source provenance

  • Robot workshop: public CameraAnything source video, all 81 decoded frames. Native 15 fps, replayed at 16 fps for comparison. We do not use their multi-shot result as input or copy its camera cuts.
  • Camel enclosure: public CameraAnything camel source, all 81 decoded frames at 16 fps. Our continuous camera path is independent of their comparison trajectory.
  • Bear among rocks / Black swan on water: DAVIS official downloads, 2017 trainval 480p archive. Bear: first 81 of 82 ordered JPEGs; black swan: first 49 of 50. Image sequences are encoded at 16 fps; this is a declared display rate, not a recovered original capture rate. Frames retain their original order; none are repeated or interpolated.

Every input is independently reconstructed with depth, optical flow, point tracks and camera fitting. Requested and renderer-recorded cameras are shown, rather than poses recovered from the generated output. The output retains the standard first-ten-source-frame conditioning of the dynamic pipeline. Unseen surfaces, animal anatomy, reflections, mechanical details and backgrounds can be approximate. These qualitative demos are not DAVIS segmentation scores or an equal-protocol benchmark against the source projects.

DAVIS: Perazzi et al., A Benchmark Dataset and Evaluation Methodology for Video Object Segmentation, CVPR 2016. CameraAnything project. Related camera-design references.
